# Abstract

**Abstract:** The context defines the shift toward immersive digital learning tools in
higher education to replace passive 2D instruction. The brief literature summarises
current Extended Reality (XR) literature, highlighting strong immediate engagement but
limited long-term evaluation. The problem statement states the central problem regarding
single-institution pilot sample sizes and the lack of theoretical knowledge retention
data. The aim introduces the Scalable, Evidence-Based XR Integration Framework
(SEB-XRIF), designed to track engagement and retention. The methodology states the
adoption of the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM). The dataset explains the use
of the xAPI Educational Mining Dataset (480 records, 17 attributes) acquired from Kaggle.
The methods specify the machine learning model selected (Random Forest Classifier) for
student performance tiering. The evaluation metrics list the performance metrics
utilised, including F1-score, System Usability Scale (SUS), and Cohen's d effect size.
Anticipated results state the expected outcomes regarding predictive classification
accuracy and long-term knowledge decay tracking. The research contribution and impact
outline the broader contribution of providing higher education institutions with a
scalable, evidence-based XR adoption framework.

**Keywords:** Extended Reality, Immersive Learning, Educational Data Mining, Design
Science Research, Learning Analytics, Reproducibility.
